DFARS 2027
The Clock Is Running

On January 1, 2027, every defense contractor in America faces a hard deadline: zero Chinese-origin rare earth magnets in any covered weapons system. For Gulf Coast contractors near Eglin, Hurlburt, Tyndall, and Duke Field — the window to qualify domestic suppliers is closing faster than most realize.

Gulf Coast Critical Materials  ·  Research Brief  ·  For Defense Procurement & Supply Chain Teams
⚠ Compliance Deadline

DFARS 252.225-7052 takes full effect January 1, 2027. Under 10 U.S.C. §4872, covered magnets are prohibited if any stage of production — mining, refining, separation, melting, or fabrication — occurred in China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea. The previous workaround of melting Chinese oxides in a third country no longer applies. Contracts without compliant supply chains face termination risk.

01 Why This Matters Here

Why This Hits Hard in the Gulf Coast

The Eglin–Hurlburt–Tyndall corridor hosts some of the most advanced weapons testing, special operations support, and aerospace systems work in the country. The platforms operating out of these installations — F-35s, AC-130s, advanced munitions systems — are precisely the weapons programs DFARS 252.225-7052 is designed to protect.

That means the prime contractors and subcontractors serving these bases are directly in the compliance crosshairs. Many mid-tier suppliers have not yet audited their magnet supply chains. Some don't know they need to.

02 Scope of the Rule

What the Rule Actually Covers

Contractors often underestimate the scope. DFARS 252.225-7052 covers four material categories — not just the finished magnet, but every upstream input:

Covered Material Common Defense Application Exposure
Neodymium-Iron-Boron (NdFeB) Magnets Missile guidance, actuators, EW systems, motors ● HIGH
Samarium-Cobalt (SmCo) Magnets High-temp aerospace, radar, avionics ● HIGH
Tantalum Metals & Alloys Capacitors, munitions components ● MEDIUM
Tungsten Heavy Alloys & Powder Penetrators, armor, counterweights ● MEDIUM

The "Third Country" Loophole Is Gone

Prior to the 2024 final rule, a contractor could source Chinese rare earth oxides, ship them to Vietnam or Japan for magnet production, and claim compliance. The updated DFARS now traces origin all the way back to the mine. If Chinese ore or processing touched the material at any point, it fails.

The COTS Exception Is Narrow

Commercial off-the-shelf items are exempt only if restricted material comprises less than 50 percent of the item by weight — a bar most magnet-containing assemblies cannot clear.

The Heavy REE Gap

Dysprosium and terbium — required for high-performance defense magnets in extreme heat — remain almost entirely Chinese-sourced at commercial scale. The USGS 2026 assessment confirms 100% import reliance for these elements with no domestic commercial-scale separation before 2028. Contractors relying on heavy REE magnets should engage DoD waiver processes now rather than waiting for the deadline.

05 Recommended Actions

What Procurement Teams Should Do Now
  • 1
    Map your magnet supply chain to the mine. Identify every NdFeB and SmCo magnet in your covered programs. Trace each to its manufacturer, then request country-of-origin documentation for ore, processing, and production. Most suppliers won't have it ready.
  • 2
    Request DFARS compliance certifications from every magnet supplier. Be specific: you need 252.225-7052 compliance confirmation, not a "Made in USA" claim. The location of final assembly is not sufficient.
  • 3
    Open conversations with at least two domestic qualified suppliers. USA Rare Earth and MP Materials both have defense BD teams. Lynas has a U.S. office. Lead times for qualification and first delivery can run 6–12 months — there is no time to wait until Q4 2026.
  • 4
    Identify programs that may require waivers. Heavy REE magnets have no commercial domestic source before 2028. Programs relying on dysprosium or terbium content should begin DoD waiver documentation now. Early applicants have better odds.
  • 5
    Engage the Eglin Small Business Office. (850) 882-2843 · 205 West D Ave, Suite 449, Eglin AFB, FL 32542. Their mandate includes connecting primes with compliant supply chain partners. They can facilitate introductions that would otherwise take months to build.
