Smith-Mundt Modernization Act Signed — Government Propaganda Permitted Domestically
Media Executive

Obama signed the National Defense Authorization Act for FY2013, which included the Smith-Mundt Modernization Act of 2012. The original Smith-Mundt Act of 1948 had prohibited the State Department and Broadcasting Board of Governors from directing government-produced propaganda at domestic American audiences — a protection put in place specifically to prevent the government from using Cold War information warfare tools on its own citizens. The 2012 amendment repealed that prohibition. Government-produced media content, including material originally intended for foreign audiences, could now legally be directed at Americans. The change received almost no mainstream media coverage at the time.

Sources: Smith-Mundt Modernization Act of 2012 (Section 1078 of NDAA FY2013) · Public Law 112-239 · Original Smith-Mundt Act, 22 U.S.C. §1461
Cost: Permanent removal of 64-year prohibition on domestic government propaganda · Legal framework for government-directed domestic information operations now in place
2013 –2016
Documented
DOJ Secretly Subpoenas AP Phone Records — 20 Reporters Surveilled
Media Surveillance Legal

In 2013 the DOJ secretly obtained two months of phone records from over 20 Associated Press reporters and editors — one of the largest known government seizures of media records. The DOJ also named Fox News reporter James Rosen as a criminal co-conspirator in a leak investigation, which required a judge to sign off on surveilling a journalist. The Obama administration prosecuted more leak cases under the Espionage Act than all previous administrations combined — eight cases total vs. three in all prior history.

Sources: AP statement on subpoena (May 2013) · DOJ Rosen affidavit · Committee to Protect Journalists report "The Obama Administration and the Press" (2013)
Cost: Chilling effect on press freedom · CPJ called Obama administration "most aggressive" against press in modern history

Jun 2013
Documented
NSA Mass Surveillance of American Citizens Revealed — Snowden Disclosures
Surveillance Legal

NSA contractor Edward Snowden disclosed classified documents revealing the NSA's PRISM program — bulk collection of internet communications from major U.S. tech companies — and Section 215 bulk phone metadata collection covering virtually all Americans. Director of National Intelligence James Clapper had testified to Congress in March 2013 that the NSA did "not wittingly" collect data on millions of Americans — a statement later acknowledged to be false. A federal appeals court later ruled the phone metadata program illegal. The Obama administration had expanded these programs inherited from Bush.

Sources: Guardian/Washington Post PRISM reporting (June 2013) · Second Circuit ruling ACLU v. Clapper (2015) · USA FREEDOM Act (2015) · Clapper Senate testimony retraction
Cost: Mass surveillance of all Americans without individual warrants · DNI Clapper lied to Congress, never prosecuted · Programs partially curtailed by USA FREEDOM Act
2011 –2016
Documented
NSA "About" Queries Violated Rules — Obama DOJ Acknowledged in 2017
Surveillance Legal

Declassified FISA Court opinions released in 2017 revealed that the NSA had conducted "about" queries — searches collecting communications that merely mentioned a target, not just communications to or from a target — in violation of FISA Court-approved procedures. The violations affected an unknown number of Americans. The FISA Court called the violations "a very serious Fourth Amendment issue." The NSA eventually terminated the collection method in 2017.

Sources: FISA Court opinion (declassified Oct 2017) · NSA announcement terminating "about" collection · Director Coats statement April 2017
Cost: Unconstitutional collection acknowledged · No individuals prosecuted for the violations

IRS Targets Conservative and Tea Party Organizations for Heightened Scrutiny
Legal Executive

The IRS subjected conservative and Tea Party organizations applying for tax-exempt status to extraordinary delays and invasive questioning — requesting donor lists, social media passwords, and details of members' personal beliefs and prayers. The DOJ Inspector General confirmed the targeting. IRS official Lois Lerner invoked the Fifth Amendment before Congress and was held in contempt. The DOJ ultimately declined to prosecute. The IRS apologized and the agency paid $3.5 million to settle lawsuits with Tea Party groups. No Obama administration official was prosecuted.

Sources: Treasury IG Report TIGTA 2013-10-053 · Lerner contempt citation · DOJ settlement · Senate Finance Committee report
Cost: Conservative groups denied tax-exempt status during 2012 election cycle · $3.5M settlement · No criminal accountability
2009 –2011
Documented
Operation Fast and Furious — ATF Walked Guns to Mexican Cartels
Legal Executive

The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF), operating under the Justice Department, ran a gun-walking operation called Fast and Furious that allowed over 2,000 firearms to be purchased by straw buyers and trafficked to Mexican drug cartels. The stated intent was to trace the weapons to cartel leadership. The weapons were lost. At least two were found at the scene of the murder of U.S. Border Patrol Agent Brian Terry in December 2010. AG Eric Holder was held in contempt of Congress for refusing to turn over documents — the first sitting cabinet member held in contempt. Obama invoked executive privilege to shield the documents.

Sources: DOJ IG Report on Fast and Furious (Sept 2012) · Holder contempt citation · House Oversight Committee report · Brian Terry Foundation
Cost: Border Patrol Agent Brian Terry killed · 2,000+ weapons lost to cartels · AG Holder held in contempt · Executive privilege invoked to block investigation
2013 –2016
Documented
Operation Choke Point — DOJ Pressures Banks to Deny Service to Legal Businesses
Legal Economic

The DOJ under AG Holder launched Operation Choke Point, which pressured banks and payment processors to terminate relationships with legal businesses the administration considered undesirable — including gun dealers, ammunition sellers, payday lenders, and other legal industries. Banks were threatened with federal scrutiny if they continued serving these customers. The FDIC issued guidance listing these industries as "high risk." A House Oversight Committee investigation found the program was designed to target legal businesses without legislative authority. The program was officially ended in 2017 under the Trump administration.

Sources: House Oversight Committee report on Operation Choke Point (May 2014) · FDIC guidance documents · DOJ termination letter (Aug 2017) · GAO report
Cost: Legal businesses denied banking services without due process · Gun dealers disproportionately targeted · No legislative authority ever established

Drone Strike Program — 563+ Strikes, Civilian Casualties Documented
Foreign Executive

The Obama administration conducted 563 drone strikes in non-battlefield countries — Pakistan, Yemen, Somalia, Libya — compared to 57 under Bush. The administration's own estimates acknowledged between 64 and 116 civilian deaths from 473 strikes in those countries between 2009–2015. Independent monitoring groups estimated civilian casualties significantly higher — the Bureau of Investigative Journalism documented between 380 and 801 civilian deaths in the same period. The program operated under secret legal memos. An American citizen, Anwar al-Awlaki, was killed by drone strike without trial in 2011. His 16-year-old American-born son was killed in a separate strike two weeks later.

Sources: Office of the Director of National Intelligence release (July 2016) · Bureau of Investigative Journalism database · ACLU v. DOJ (al-Awlaki memo) · Executive Order 13732
Cost: American citizens killed without trial · Civilian casualties disputed but documented · Secret legal framework for presidential kill list established as precedent
Jan 2016
Documented
$400 Million Cash Payment to Iran — Timed to Hostage Release
Foreign Executive

The Obama administration transferred $400 million in foreign currency on wooden pallets via unmarked cargo plane to Iran on January 17, 2016 — the same day four American hostages were released. The administration initially denied the payment was a ransom, citing it as settlement of a 1979 arms deal dispute. The Wall Street Journal reported that U.S. officials had used the cash as leverage, telling Iran the funds would not be transferred until the Americans were released. The State Department later acknowledged the payment was contingent on the hostage release. Total payments in the settlement reached $1.7 billion.

Sources: State Department acknowledgment (Aug 2016) · Wall Street Journal reporting · Treasury Department statements · Congressional Research Service
Cost: $1.7 billion total · Established precedent of cash payments concurrent with hostage releases · Administration initially misled public about the connection
Sep 2012
Documented
Benghazi — Ambassador Killed, Administration Misattributed Attack to Video
Foreign

On September 11, 2012, the U.S. consulate in Benghazi, Libya was attacked. Ambassador Chris Stevens and three other Americans were killed. Within days, UN Ambassador Susan Rice appeared on five Sunday news programs attributing the attack to a spontaneous reaction to an internet video — a characterization the administration repeated for days. Internal emails released later showed the CIA's own initial assessment had identified it as a terrorist attack. Hillary Clinton emailed her daughter the night of the attack describing it as carried out by "an al-Qaeda-like group." The State Department's own Accountability Review Board found systemic security failures. No one was held accountable within the State Department.

Sources: State Dept Accountability Review Board report (Dec 2012) · Clinton email to Chelsea (released 2015) · CIA talking points revision chain · Senate Intelligence Committee report (Jan 2014)
Cost: Four Americans killed · Public misled about cause for weeks · No disciplinary action for security failures
2015
Documented
Iran Nuclear Deal (JCPOA) — Bypassed Senate Treaty Process
Foreign Executive

The Obama administration negotiated the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action with Iran and implemented it as an executive agreement rather than a treaty, deliberately bypassing the requirement for Senate ratification by two-thirds vote. The Iran Nuclear Agreement Review Act passed by Congress required congressional review but not approval. The deal provided Iran $100 billion+ in sanctions relief and lifted arms embargoes. Critics argued it left Iran's nuclear infrastructure intact and sunset key restrictions. The deal was not submitted as a treaty and had no binding legal force on successor administrations.

Sources: JCPOA text (July 2015) · Iran Nuclear Agreement Review Act · Senate Foreign Relations Committee hearings · Congressional Research Service analysis of treaty vs. executive agreement
Cost: $100B+ in sanctions relief to Iran · Bypassed constitutional treaty process · No binding obligation on successor administrations — Trump withdrew in 2018

$535 Million Solyndra Loan Guarantee — Company Bankrupt Within Two Years
Economic Executive

The Obama administration approved a $535 million loan guarantee to solar panel manufacturer Solyndra in September 2009 — restructuring the loan terms in early 2011 to subordinate the government's claim behind private investors, contrary to federal law. Solyndra filed for bankruptcy in September 2011, costing taxpayers the full guaranteed amount. Internal emails showed White House officials had been warned of Solyndra's financial problems before approval. Solyndra's primary investor, George Kaiser, was a major Obama campaign bundler who had visited the White House 16 times.

Sources: DOE Inspector General report · House Energy and Commerce Committee investigation · Solyndra bankruptcy filings · White House visitor logs
Cost: $535 million taxpayer loss · DOE subordinated taxpayer claim illegally · Criminal investigation opened, no charges filed
2009 –2014
Documented
"If You Like Your Plan, You Can Keep Your Plan" — PolitiFact Lie of the Year
Economic Executive

President Obama repeatedly stated — at least 37 times in various forms — that under the Affordable Care Act, "if you like your health care plan, you can keep it." After the ACA took effect, millions of Americans received cancellation notices from their insurers because their plans did not meet new ACA requirements. PolitiFact named this statement its "Lie of the Year" for 2013. The administration acknowledged the reality did not match the promise and attempted a one-year administrative fix allowing some plans to continue, which insurers largely could not implement in time.

Sources: PolitiFact Lie of the Year (2013) · HHS cancellation data · Obama statement archive · NBC News "millions of cancellations" reporting (Oct 2013)
Cost: Millions of Americans lost health plans they were told they could keep · Named Lie of the Year by fact-checkers · Trust in ACA rollout severely damaged

Documented
DACA and DAPA — Executive Immigration Actions Bypassing Congress
Executive Legal

Obama implemented Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) by executive memorandum in June 2012 after Congress failed to pass the DREAM Act. In 2014 he expanded it and added Deferred Action for Parents of Americans (DAPA), which would have protected approximately 4 million undocumented immigrants from deportation. Obama had previously stated at least 22 times that he lacked the legal authority to take such action unilaterally. A federal court blocked DAPA; the Supreme Court split 4-4 leaving the block in place. Obama himself had said in 2011: "I'm not the emperor of the United States. My job is to execute laws that are passed."

Sources: DACA executive memorandum (June 15, 2012) · Obama video archive of prior statements · Texas v. United States (5th Circuit, 2015) · SCOTUS 4-4 split (2016)
Cost: Circumvented legislative process Obama himself had said he lacked authority to do · Created legal uncertainty lasting years · DAPA permanently blocked

Record Use of Recess Appointments Blocked by Courts
Executive Legal

Obama made recess appointments to the National Labor Relations Board and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau while the Senate held pro forma sessions — sessions specifically designed to prevent recess appointments. The Supreme Court ruled unanimously in NLRB v. Noel Canning (2014) that the appointments were unconstitutional. It was a 9-0 ruling against the administration's expansive interpretation of executive appointment power.

Sources: NLRB v. Noel Canning, 573 U.S. 513 (2014) · Supreme Court opinion (unanimous) · Senate pro forma session records
Cost: Unconstitutional appointments invalidated 9-0 by Supreme Court · NLRB decisions made during that period thrown into question
Dec 2016
Documented
Outgoing Administration Expands NSA Data Sharing — Days Before Trump Inauguration
Executive Surveillance

On January 3, 2017 — 17 days before Trump's inauguration — the Obama administration finalized rules allowing the NSA to share raw signals intelligence with 16 other intelligence agencies before applying privacy protections. Previously the NSA had to filter and minimize data before sharing. The new rules meant a wider pool of government analysts could access raw intercepts — including communications of Americans — before identities were masked. Critics noted the timing made it easier for information about Trump transition officials to flow across agencies.

Sources: Executive Order 12333 amendment, published Federal Register Jan 3, 2017 · New York Times reporting (Jan 12, 2017) · ODNI procedural changes
Cost: Expanded raw intelligence sharing implemented days before transition · Increased number of officials with access to unmasked American communications

Documented
U.S. Abstains on UN Resolution Condemning Israeli Settlements — Breaking 40-Year Policy
Foreign

In December 2016 — after losing the election and during the transition — the Obama administration broke with 40 years of U.S. policy by allowing UN Security Council Resolution 2334 to pass, which declared Israeli settlements in the West Bank illegal under international law. The U.S. abstained rather than vetoing, as all previous administrations had done on similar resolutions. The move was made after the election, during the lame duck period. Israel accused the Obama administration of secretly coordinating the resolution — a claim the administration denied.

Sources: UN Security Council vote record (Dec 23, 2016) · State Department explanation · Israeli government statement · Washington Post reporting on alleged coordination
Cost: 40-year U.S. policy reversed in final weeks of administration · Diplomatic damage to U.S.-Israel relationship · Made during lame duck period after electoral defeat
