Crowley County, Colorado

The Systematic Theft of Water Rights and the Death of a Rural Community

The Record

In the 1970s and 1980s, political and bureaucratic decisions in Colorado transferred water rights from family farms and ranches in Crowley County to the rapidly growing Denver metropolitan area.

This was not a natural drought. It was policy. The result was the economic and demographic collapse of an entire agricultural region that had thrived for generations.

Consequences

  • Population decline and economic devastation in Crowley County
  • Loss of multi-generational family land and heritage
  • Precedent for similar rural water reallocations across the American West

Why It Matters

This case exemplifies how ruling class institutions prioritize urban growth and political interests over rural communities and longstanding property rights.
